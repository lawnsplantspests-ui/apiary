Safety first

Before you consider apitherapy

Bee venom is not gentle, and a sting is not a low-risk event. We'd be doing you a disservice not to say this plainly:

  • Stings can cause severe allergic reactions — including anaphylaxis, which can be life-threatening — even in people who have been stung before with no problem.
  • Talk to a licensed medical provider first. Allergy testing beforehand is worth discussing with your doctor.
  • Never do it alone. Have another person present, keep emergency epinephrine (such as an EpiPen) on hand, and know the signs of a reaction and when to call 911.
  • A known bee-sting allergy makes this dangerous. If you or the person involved has ever had a serious sting reaction, do not proceed without a doctor's direct supervision.

None of this is medical advice — it's the basic caution any beekeeper would give a neighbor. Your doctor is the right person to weigh whether apitherapy makes sense for you.
